Auto-rollover and safe harbor IRAs: the account you never opened

4 min read · Updated 2026-07-25

What is an automatic rollover IRA?
An automatic rollover IRA, also called a safe harbor IRA, is an account a former employer's plan opens in your name — without needing your signature — to move a small balance out of the plan after you leave. It is legal and legitimate, but because notice goes to your last known address, many people never learn the account exists.

Why plans do it

Maintaining accounts for departed employees costs money and creates administrative duties. Federal rules let plans move small balances into an IRA that meets safe harbor conditions, which discharges much of the plan's ongoing responsibility while preserving the money's tax-advantaged status.

Why it goes wrong

Notice is sent to the address on file. People move. Safe harbor IRAs are typically invested conservatively to preserve principal, so returns are often low while account fees continue — meaning a modest balance can erode over years. And the account sits at an institution you never chose and may not recognise when a statement finally arrives.

How to find one

Ask the former employer or its recordkeeper where the balance went and to which IRA provider. Use Form 5500 filings to identify who administers the plan now if the company changed hands. Check the federal registries, and check state unclaimed property in case the IRA itself later went dormant and escheated.

What to do once you find it

You can usually consolidate it into a current employer's plan or an IRA of your choosing. That is a financial decision with tax consequences, so it is worth speaking to a licensed professional — ReclaimWealth does not give financial, tax, or legal advice.

You can claim your property yourself, for free. Your state or retirement plan does not charge you to file a claim for your own property, and every registry we reference is free to search. ReclaimWealth charges only for optional help doing the legwork.
