Claiming a deceased relative's retirement account

4 min read · Updated 2026-07-25

Can I claim a deceased parent's or spouse's 401(k) or pension?
Yes, if you are the named beneficiary or the legal heir, but you will need documentation — typically a death certificate, proof of your relationship or beneficiary status, and sometimes probate paperwork. Each plan and each state sets its own evidence requirements, and the process takes longer than claiming your own property.

Beneficiary designation usually controls

For retirement accounts, the beneficiary form on file with the plan generally governs who inherits — often overriding a will. A surviving spouse frequently has rights under federal law. If a beneficiary was named, that person claims directly from the plan.

Where to search

The PBGC unclaimed pension database is the place to start for traditional pensions, especially where the employer failed. Then state unclaimed property under the deceased person's name and every state they lived or worked in, and Form 5500 filings to find the administrator of a former employer's plan.

Documents to expect

A certified death certificate, your government ID, proof of relationship such as a marriage or birth certificate, and where there is no named beneficiary, letters testamentary or other probate documentation showing your authority to act for the estate.

Get advice on the tax treatment

Inherited retirement accounts have distribution rules and tax consequences that differ from your own accounts and that have changed in recent years. Speak to a tax professional or estate attorney before taking a distribution — this is exactly the territory where a wrong move is expensive, and ReclaimWealth does not give tax or legal advice.

You can claim your property yourself, for free. Your state or retirement plan does not charge you to file a claim for your own property, and every registry we reference is free to search. ReclaimWealth charges only for optional help doing the legwork.
