How to find a lost 401(k): the complete process

6 min read · Updated 2026-07-25

How do I find a lost 401(k) from an old job?
Search four places in order: the Department of Labor's Retirement Savings Lost & Found, the National Registry of Unclaimed Retirement Benefits, your state's unclaimed property program, and the DOL's Form 5500 filings to identify who administers your old plan today. All four are free, and no legitimate service can look up your private balance by name.

Before you start: gather what you can remember

Every employer where you may have had a plan, the years you worked there, the states you worked in, and any names you have used — maiden names and former names matter, because records are indexed by the name on file at the time. You do not need account numbers to begin.

1. DOL Retirement Savings Lost & Found

The federal database of plans looking for former participants, built from what plans report to the IRS and created by the SECURE 2.0 Act. Be ready for the identity check: it uses Login.gov and needs your Social Security number, date of birth, and photos of a government ID. It does not cover church or government plans, and it is not comprehensive — a 'nothing found' result is not proof you have no lost accounts.

2. National Registry of Unclaimed Retirement Benefits

A private registry where participating employers report unclaimed accounts. It needs your Social Security number and only covers employers who chose to register, so treat it as a quick extra check rather than an authoritative answer.

3. State unclaimed property

The easiest place to start, because it needs only your name — no identity verification. If a retirement balance was cashed out and went dormant, it may sit with a state treasurer. Search every state you have lived or worked in, not just where you live now.

4. Form 5500 filings — the step most people skip

This is the professional's tool. Employer plans file annual Form 5500 reports that name the plan administrator and sponsor. When a company merged, was acquired, or closed, the filing tells you who is legally responsible for the plan today — which is the party you actually need to call. It is free, it is public, and it needs no personal information.

If a plan will not respond

Contact a Department of Labor Benefits Advisor through EBSA. They help former participants for free and can intervene when a plan is unresponsive. Almost nobody uses this, and it is one of the most effective escalations available.

You can claim your property yourself, for free. Your state or retirement plan does not charge you to file a claim for your own property, and every registry we reference is free to search. ReclaimWealth charges only for optional help doing the legwork.
