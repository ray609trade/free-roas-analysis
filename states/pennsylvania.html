Find unclaimed 401(k) & retirement money in Pennsylvania

If you worked in Pennsylvania and left a job behind, you may also have left a retirement account behind. Pennsylvania holds unclaimed property through the Pennsylvania Unclaimed Property, administered by the Pennsylvania Treasury — and searching it is free and requires only your name.

You can claim your property yourself, for free. Pennsylvania does not charge you to file a claim for your own property, and every registry we reference is free to search. ReclaimWealth charges only for optional help doing the legwork.

What's specific to Pennsylvania

  • Pennsylvania Treasury operates a formal Finder certification program.
  • Fees are capped at 15% of the value recovered.
  • For paid recovery help, registration with the state is required for paid finders, finder fees are capped at 15%, agreements may be restricted for 24 months after property is reported.

Searching Pennsylvania is only half the job

State unclaimed property only holds accounts that were already cashed out and escheated. A 401(k) still sitting with your old employer's plan will not appear there. To find those, search the federal registries too:

  1. DOL Retirement Savings Lost & Found — Private-sector employer and union retirement plans, built from information plans report to the IRS. Requires: Identity verification through Login.gov: your legal name, date of birth, Social Security number, a mobile device, and photos of the front and back of a valid driver's license or state ID.
  2. National Registry of Unclaimed Retirement Benefits — Retirement plan benefits that participating employers have voluntarily reported as unclaimed. Requires: Your Social Security number to run a search.
  3. PBGC Unclaimed Pensions — Unclaimed benefits from traditional defined benefit pension plans that PBGC has taken over, typically after the sponsoring company failed. Requires: Name and, helpfully, the former employer's name. No SSN needed for the initial search.
  4. State Unclaimed Property (MissingMoney) — Cash and securities turned over to states when accounts go dormant, including cashed-out retirement balances. Requires: Just your name. This is public record — no SSN required to search.
  5. DOL Form 5500 / EFAST Filing Search — Annual reports filed by employer retirement plans, which name the plan administrator and plan sponsor contacts. Requires: The former employer's name. No personal information needed.

Common questions about Pennsylvania unclaimed retirement money

How do I search for unclaimed retirement money in Pennsylvania?

Search the Pennsylvania Unclaimed Property operated by the Pennsylvania Treasury at https://www.patreasury.gov/unclaimed-property/. It is free, it is public record, and it requires only your name — no Social Security number. Search every name you have used, including maiden and former names.

Does Pennsylvania charge a fee to claim my unclaimed property?

No. Pennsylvania does not charge you to claim your own property, and you never need to pay anyone to file a claim. Paid services, including ours, sell convenience and legwork only — the underlying claim is always free to file yourself.

How do I find a 401(k) from an old job?

Search four places in order: the Department of Labor's Retirement Savings Lost & Found, the National Registry of Unclaimed Retirement Benefits, your state's unclaimed property program, and the DOL's Form 5500 filings to identify your old plan's administrator. All four are free to search.

Is searching for a lost 401(k) free?

Yes. Every official registry is free to search, and you can file a claim for your own money at no cost. ReclaimWealth charges only for optional assistance doing the work for you — never for access to your own funds.

Can a service look up my 401(k) balance by name?

No, and you should be sceptical of anyone claiming otherwise. Private retirement balances are protected by law and cannot be retrieved by name alone. Official registries like the DOL Lost & Found require you to verify your own identity through Login.gov using your Social Security number and a government-issued ID.

How much money is sitting in forgotten 401(k) accounts?

An estimated $2.1 trillion sits in about 31.9 million forgotten 401(k) accounts in the United States, with an average balance of roughly $66,691 — around a quarter of all 401(k) assets. That figure comes from 2025 research by Capitalize with the Center for Retirement Research at Boston College.
