What happens to your 401(k) when you leave a job

4 min read · Updated 2026-07-25

What happens to a 401(k) when you leave a job?
When you leave a job your 401(k) does not disappear or revert to your employer — the money stays yours. It normally remains in the old employer's plan, but depending on the balance it may be cashed out to you, rolled into an IRA chosen by the plan, or eventually turned over to your state as unclaimed property.

The balance decides what happens

Federal rules let plans treat small balances differently from large ones. Broadly: larger balances stay put unless you move them, mid-size balances can be moved into an IRA without your signature, and very small balances can simply be cashed out and mailed to you. The exact thresholds are set by regulation and have been raised over time, so check with the plan rather than relying on a number you read years ago.

The 'force-out' is what catches people

If your balance is above the cash-out floor but below the force-out ceiling, the plan may move your money into an IRA it selects for you — without needing your consent. This is a legitimate, legal process called an automatic rollover or safe harbor IRA. The problem is practical: the notice goes to the address the plan has on file. If you moved, you never see it, and years later you have an IRA at an institution you have never heard of.

Then the trail goes cold

Those auto-rollover IRAs are often parked in conservative, low-return options while fees continue to accrue. If mail keeps bouncing and the account goes dormant, the funds may eventually be escheated to your state as unclaimed property — which is why a 401(k) from a job you left can end up findable through a state treasurer rather than through any retirement plan at all.

What to do about it

Work backwards through the same chain: check the old employer's plan and its recordkeeper first, then the federal registries, then state unclaimed property for every state you have lived or worked in. All of these are free to search, and you can file any claim yourself at no cost.

You can claim your property yourself, for free. Your state or retirement plan does not charge you to file a claim for your own property, and every registry we reference is free to search. ReclaimWealth charges only for optional help doing the legwork.
