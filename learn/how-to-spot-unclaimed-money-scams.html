How to spot an unclaimed money scam

3 min read · Updated 2026-07-25

How do I know if an unclaimed money service is a scam?
The clearest warning sign is anyone claiming to have already located money for you before you have verified your identity with the holding institution — private retirement balances cannot be looked up by name, so nobody can honestly know. Others: demands for payment by gift card or wire, claims of government affiliation, and manufactured urgency about money that is in fact held indefinitely.

Nobody can see your balance by name

Private retirement account balances are protected. Official registries such as the DOL Lost & Found require you to verify your own identity through Login.gov. Any service claiming to have retrieved your balance from your name alone is either mistaken or lying.

Warning signs

Requests for your Social Security number or bank login by email or text. Anyone claiming to be a government agency while demanding a fee. Any warning that your money will vanish unless you respond immediately — in most states unclaimed property is held indefinitely. Payment demanded by gift card, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency. Refusal to state a fee in writing before you agree.

What legitimate help looks like

Fees disclosed in writing up front. A clear statement that you can search and claim for free yourself. No custody of your funds — money moves from the institution or state directly to you. Willingness to explain exactly which records were searched, and no pressure.

Know your rights on fees

Many states regulate paid recovery assistance, including registration requirements, caps on fees, and restrictions on agreements made soon after property is reported to the state. In several states an agreement that breaks those rules is void and unenforceable. If someone wants a large percentage of your money, check your state's rules before signing anything.
