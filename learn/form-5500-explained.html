Form 5500: how to find out who holds your old plan

4 min read · Updated 2026-07-25

How do I find out who administers my former employer's 401(k) plan?
Search the Department of Labor's Form 5500 filings. Employer retirement plans file an annual report that names the plan sponsor and administrator with contact details, so even when a company has merged, been acquired, or closed, the filing shows who is responsible for the plan today. It is free, public, and requires no personal information.

What Form 5500 is

An annual report most employer-sponsored retirement plans must file with the federal government, covering the plan's finances, participants, and operations. The filings are public and searchable through the DOL's EFAST system.

What you can learn from it

The legal plan name, the sponsor's name and EIN, the plan administrator and their address, the number of participants, and the plan year. Where a company was acquired, later filings often reveal the successor — the thread that reconnects you to your account.

How to search effectively

Search the employer's exact legal name, not the brand you remember, and try variations including former names and 'Inc' or 'LLC' suffixes. Look at filings from the years you worked there, then follow forward to the most recent filing to see who holds the plan now.

Its limits

Very small plans have reduced filing obligations, and filings can lag a year or more. If nothing appears, the plan may have terminated — in which case the balance was likely distributed, rolled into an IRA, or escheated to a state.
