The hidden problem

People leave retirement money behind all the time — often without knowing it.

When you change jobs, a small 401(k) balance can get automatically rolled into an IRA, sent to the state as unclaimed property, or simply left sitting with a plan you've forgotten. Years later, it's still yours — you just have to find it.

  • ✓ You had a 401(k) at a job you left years ago
  • ✓ A former employer merged, was acquired, or went out of business
  • ✓ You moved and never updated your address with an old plan
  • ✓ You had a pension from a company or union in the past
  • ✓ You were part of a partnership or firm with a retirement benefit

Where We Search

We guide your search through the official, government-backed and national registries where lost and abandoned retirement accounts are reported.

🏛️

DOL Retirement Savings Lost & Found

The federal Department of Labor database of retirement plans searching for their participants.

📋

National Registry of Unclaimed Retirement Benefits

A secure registry that helps former employees locate benefits left behind in retirement plans.

💼

PBGC Unclaimed Pensions

The Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation's database of unclaimed pension benefits.

🗺️

State Unclaimed Property (MissingMoney)

Official state treasuries where escheated retirement funds and cash are reported.

🔎

DOL Form 5500 / EFAST

Federal filings used to trace the current administrator of your old employer's retirement plan.

🤝

Plan Administrator Outreach

We help you contact the recordkeeper holding your old account so you can restart your login and claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this really free?

Yes — searching for your accounts is completely free. The registries we point you to are free public and government resources. We help simplify and organize the search so you don't have to hunt through each database yourself.

Do you have direct access to my account balances?

No — and no legitimate service does. Your private balances are protected by law. What we do is check the official registries where lost and abandoned accounts are reported, and trace your former employers' plan administrators, so you can then verify your identity directly with the institution and reclaim your money.

Why do you need my former employers?

Retirement accounts are tied to the employer that sponsored them. Knowing where you worked lets us trace the right plan and its current administrator — even if the company merged, was bought, or closed.

Is my information safe?

We only collect what's needed to help your search, we never sell your personal information, and we don't ask for your Social Security number just to get started. You stay in control of what you share.

What if you don't find anything?

Then you've lost nothing but a minute of your time — and you'll have peace of mind. Many people do have money waiting, and it costs nothing to check.

Your old retirement money isn't going anywhere. But you should go get it.

It takes about a minute to search. If there's money out there with your name on it, we'll help you find it.
