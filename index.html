Trusted sources

Where we search

These are the official registries where lost and abandoned retirement accounts are reported. Every one is free, and every one is linked here so you can go straight to it.

🏛️

DOL Retirement Savings Lost & Found

The federal Department of Labor database of plans searching for former participants. Requires identity verification through Login.gov. Does not cover church or government plans.

📋

National Registry of Unclaimed Retirement Benefits

A registry employers use to report unclaimed accounts. Searching requires your Social Security number and covers only plans that registered with it.

💼

PBGC Unclaimed Pensions

The Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation's database of unclaimed traditional pension benefits.

🗺️

State Unclaimed Property (MissingMoney)

Official state treasuries holding escheated funds. Public record, free to search. See our state-by-state guides.

🔎

DOL Form 5500 / EFAST Search

Federal filings that identify the administrator still responsible for a former employer's plan — even after a merger or closure.

🤝

DOL Benefits Advisors

Free federal help from the Employee Benefits Security Administration if a plan won't respond or you can't locate an administrator.

Straight answers

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a 401(k) from an old job?

Search four places in order: the Department of Labor's Retirement Savings Lost & Found, the National Registry of Unclaimed Retirement Benefits, your state's unclaimed property program, and the DOL's Form 5500 filings to identify your old plan's administrator. All four are free to search. The free search plan above organizes all four for your specific work history.

Is searching for a lost 401(k) free?

Yes. Every official registry is free to search, and you can file a claim for your own money at no cost. ReclaimWealth charges only for optional assistance doing the work for you — never for access to your own funds.

Can a service look up my 401(k) balance by name?

No — and be sceptical of anyone claiming otherwise. Private retirement balances are protected by law and cannot be retrieved by name alone. Official registries like the DOL Lost & Found require you to verify your identity through Login.gov with your Social Security number and a government-issued ID. What a legitimate service does is organize the search, trace your old plans, and guide the claim.

How much money is sitting in forgotten 401(k) accounts?

An estimated $2.1 trillion sits in about 31.9 million forgotten 401(k) accounts in the U.S., with an average balance of roughly $66,691 — around a quarter of all 401(k) assets. That's from 2025 research by Capitalize with the Center for Retirement Research at Boston College.1

What happens to a 401(k) when you leave a job?

It usually stays in the employer's plan. Smaller balances may be automatically rolled into an IRA, and if the account goes dormant and the plan can't locate you, the funds may eventually be turned over to your state as unclaimed property. In every one of those cases the money is still yours.

Does the DOL Lost & Found cover every plan?

No. It draws on what private-sector employers and unions report to the IRS, and it excludes church and government plans. It's also not comprehensive — a "nothing found" result doesn't mean you have no lost accounts, which is exactly why the state and Form 5500 searches matter.

Why do you ask for my former employers?

Retirement accounts are tied to the employer that sponsored the plan. Knowing where you worked lets us trace the correct plan and its current administrator through Form 5500 filings — even if the company merged, was bought, or went out of business.

Is my information safe?

We collect only what's needed to run your search, we never sell your personal information, and we don't ask for your Social Security number to get started. See our Privacy Policy for specifics on what we keep and for how long.
